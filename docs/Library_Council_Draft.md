# The OpenShelf Library Council

## Overview

The Library Council is the community-driven curation body responsible for maintaining the quality and accuracy of OpenShelf's taxonomy. Rather than allowing unchecked crowdsourced tagging, the Council acts as a specialized review board to evaluate, promote, and moderate user-submitted tags—specifically focusing on Thematic Tags.

## How the Council Works

### Genre Expertise & Tag Promotion
Library Council members are assigned to specific genres based on their proven expertise. Their primary role is to monitor user submissions within their domains and promote valuable, accurate, and highly relevant genre-specific tags. 

Additionally, as part of our evolving taxonomy rules, if a specific genre tag begins appearing consistently across 3 or more different genres, it becomes eligible to be promoted to a broader Thematic Tag.

### The Weekly Voting Cycle
1. **Submission & Promotion**: Throughout the week, OpenShelf users submit tag suggestions. Users can submit up to three tags simultaneously (one for each taxonomy tier) and can even suggest tags for books not yet in the database by providing the Title and ISBN. Council members review these submissions within their designated genres and promote the best candidates for a wider vote.
2. **Voting**: Once a week, all promoted Thematic Tags are put to a vote by all Council members.
3. **Transparency & Vetoes**: The results of the weekly voting are publicly posted. If a tag is vetoed by the Council, it must be accompanied by a written explanation to maintain transparency and guide future user submissions.
4. **Backend Updates**: Following the conclusion of the weekly vote, approved tags are compiled into a new tag dump, and the backend is updated accordingly.

## Joining the Library Council

The Council is made up of dedicated readers, authors, and metadata enthusiasts with proven domain knowledge. 

To join the Library Council during our initial launch phase, please submit an application via email. Your application should include:
- A brief introduction about yourself and your reading habits.
- Your requested genre(s) of expertise.
- Evidence of your expertise in these genres (e.g., your reading history, blog, Goodreads profile, professional background, or community involvement).
